A recap of the summer so far ☀️

This blog has been running for just over 2 months now, so I figured it might make sense to post a recap of what’s been going on. Mainly for my own benefit, but you’re welcome to read along!

  • The biggest event of the summer so far was definitely moving house, from our home of many years to a temporary flat further east, bringing with it new views and a new commute.

  • Travel has been limited to a day trip to Brighton and 2 weeks in Sweden for holiday and remote working, with an enjoyable trip to the island Klädesholmen up the coast, as well as a bit of boating.

  • Culturally, it’s been a pretty decent period. I’ve been to V&A East, The Serpentine Pavilion, Tate modern, Pilane Sculpture Park in Sweden, The Royal Academy Summer Exhibition, NIGO at the Design Museum, The Quentin Blake Centre for Illustration and Anish Kapoor at Hayward Gallery. I’ve also attended two Proms at Royal Albert Hall.

  • Personal projects have obviously involved the blog itself, which I launched on 24 May, using AI to build my own static site generator. I’ve also been playing around with creating some signal flag icons as well as a rainbow blow logo for a different kind of signalling. I also started a reboot of my wardrobe, which I need to get back to.

  • Life stuff hasn’t been too dramatic, but I did a write-up of my values as well as a life-tracker to keep my focus. My birthday is coming up, so it’s likely I will soon start creating a plan for what my next year might look like.

  • Financially, I’ve been getting used to the higher expenses in the new place, including a hike in travel costs.

  • What I bought has been limited to day-to-day items, with nothing new or exciting to report, except for one thing I will post about when it arrives.

  • Most unexpected has been the weather. Where’s the rain gone? And it’s been hot pretty much every day. I feel ready for autumn!

All in all, so far, so good! 😊

My values

I like systems and processes, and easily end up with long lists of specific rules, goals and principles for how to live my life.

However, as much as I enjoy creating these systems, they quickly lose their appeal. Long lists feel like pressure. Too many rules hold me back, or I end up ignoring them. And when I do, the system breaks down.

A couple of years ago, inspired by some article or podcast I can’t remember now, I tried a different approach: identifying my personal values.

Much to my surprise, it has actually been working! I have stuck to them since and never felt a need to rewrite them. Unlike the long list of life rules, I know them by heart, and they don’t get outdated the same way the more granular items do.

Whenever I have a decision to make, I avoid things that don’t align with my values. If I feel discontent, I use my values to try to find what’s missing.

Sure, I may still relapse and create a list of intentions once in a while, but when I do, I now have my values to guide me.

But enough background, let’s move on to the values themselves!

My Values

  • Happiness
  • Simplicity
  • Intentionality
  • Curiosity
  • Being a Good Citizen

Memento mori

Life tracker

Something happened a few months ago that forced me to reflect on my own mortality. All is good now (I hope!), but it was a reminder that it will not always be the case.

It made me think of a quote at the top of one of my favourite pages on the early Internet, Mark Rosenstein’s Sailing Page:

“For the truth is that I already know as much about my fate as I need to know. The day will come when I will die. So the only matter of consequence before me is what I will do with my allotted time. I can remain on shore, paralyzed with fear, or I can raise my sails and dip and soar in the breeze.”

– Richard Bode, First you have to row a little boat

I wish I could say that’s how I’ve been living since I read it (I even read the book), but convenience and routine came between.

I wouldn’t say I’ve wasted time, per se. I realised during Covid that it’s the little things you do every day that add up to a life, and I’m definitely living those. Nevertheless, in the spirit of appreciating those moments even more, and maybe adding in a few more bits, I have now created a life tracker.

This is inspired by a post I saw on Pinterest in terms of the format, and shows how many weeks I’ve used up and how many I may have left, assuming I live as long as the average man of my age in the UK.

The tracker is updated automatically, based on today’s date, so the clock is ticking.

Food for thought, when you see it like this.
