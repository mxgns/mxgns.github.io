Anish Kapoor at Hayward Gallery

I always enjoy Anish Kapoor’s art, and his latest exhibition was a treat.

Much of the theme this time was red blobs. A big inflated red blob filling most of a gallery and the one next to it. A big lava-like blob hanging from the ceiling. Other big red organic-looking ones in shrinkwrap.

I’m not sure I’m doing it justice…

Out on the balcony, there were two of his trademark big, bent mirrors, and those are always fascinating.

But what really caught my interest was a room with what at first sight seemed to be painted black shapes on flat white walls. Once you got closer, though, what looked like just a black ellipse painted on the wall turned out to be, in fact, part of a white half-sphere coming out of the wall. What looked like just a black square turned out to have a black ball hanging in front.

Again, I don’t think I’m doing it justice, but as far as optical illusions go, these were some of the more fascinating I’ve seen.

“I’ve never seen anything so totally black before”, I thought to myself, and it turned out to be true: the black Vantablack coating is apparently the blackest substance known to man. This was a very good use of it.
